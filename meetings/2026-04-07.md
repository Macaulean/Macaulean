## Summary

The team met to discuss progress on their extended abstract for a CS journal submission due April 11th. Jay shared the current draft, which included 3 pages of content and half a page of references, and outlined plans to complete a content draft by Thursday with final review on Friday before Saturday submission. ## Topics

### Tactics and Polynomial Branch Updates

### Polynomial Representation System Development

The team discussed the development of a polynomial representation system, with Jay explaining that the current poly wrapper needs significant rewrite and that different polynomial representations exist across their codebase. Damiano agreed they should develop their own polynomial representation type rather than relying on Grind, as it's not suitable for their scale of computations. Jay outlined his approach of representing polynomials as expressions using terms (coefficients and monomials) with products and sums, rather than immediately storing expanded polynomials, to avoid expensive computation steps. Damiano suggested that Macaulay 2 should track modifications made to the input to help Lean generate proof terms, requiring a detailed certificate system.

### Macaulay 2 Integration Planning

The team discussed the operations that Macaulay 2 can perform and how to make them accessible for integration with lean. Jay explained that while Macaulay 2 has many internal operations, they could start by focusing on easily accessible operations and potentially logging intermediate steps for operations like polynomial multiplication. Michael provided details about how Macaulay 2 represents polynomials, explaining that it uses a flattened polynomial structure as a list of terms within a defined ring with a specified term order, which was chosen to differ from Maple's approach.

### Polynomial Representation in Macaulay 2

Michael and Damiano discussed the internal representation and operations of polynomials in Macaulay 2, focusing on how coefficients and monomials are structured and handled. They addressed challenges with normal forms, particularly for fraction fields over non-polynomial rings, and mentioned ongoing work to improve the field notion with Frank Moore. The discussion highlighted the complexity of handling polynomials with coefficients from various rings and the potential benefits of exploiting initial structure for optimization, though challenges remain in determining lead terms and handling zero polynomials.

### Polynomial Computation Challenges in Lean

The group discussed challenges in computing the degree of polynomials, particularly when dealing with zero rings and different coefficient rings. Jay proposed viewing polynomial operations as vector space operations, suggesting a matrix representation for multiplication that could simplify computations. Damiano and Jay agreed that working with a single infinite-dimensional vector space of all monomials might be more straightforward in Lean than dealing with multiple intermediate vector spaces. The discussion highlighted a tension between ease of implementation in Lean and practical considerations for serialization and transfer of data.

### Rebasing and CAS Integration Discussion

The team discussed rebasing an extended abstract branch, with Jay working to fix issues with the branch being based on the wrong starting point. Damiano mentioned other groups working on CAS integration in Lean, particularly with Sage, though performance details were unclear. The conversation concluded with a discussion about the challenges of depending on Mathlib, as polynomial implementations diverge from current Mathlib versions, making them incompatible over time.

The conversation briefly touched on the differences between CS journal and mathematics paper standards, with Damiano sharing his positive experience with CS publications.

Jay explained how he used this system to make the code more standard and modular, allowing for easy addition of future options.
