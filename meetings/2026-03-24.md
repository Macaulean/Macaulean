## Summary

The team met to discuss progress on polynomial equality proofs and serialization between Lean and Macaulay 2. Jay demonstrated his work on representing polynomials as sorted lists of terms and showed how he could handle coefficients by converting them to integers when the ring type is unknown. The team discussed performance issues with proof construction in Lean, particularly around polynomial multiplication and expansion operations, with Jay noting that the current approach creates enormous expressions that slow down the kernel. They explored potential solutions including breaking proofs into smaller steps, using monomial-by-polynomial multiplication, and proving polynomial equality by checking lead terms. The group also briefly discussed an upcoming ICMS extended abstract submission deadline and agreed to contribute content about their infrastructure and architecture progress.

## Topics

### Polynomial Handling Implementation Progress

Jay demonstrated recent progress on polynomial handling, showing how he implemented a fallback mechanism to use integers (Z) when dealing with unknown rings. He explained that the implementation works by first attempting to serialize polynomials in their intended ring, but falls back to using integers if coefficient handling fails. Jay also noted improvements in handling negative coefficients and natural numbers, which now get converted to actual NAT types rather than being treated as unknown coefficients. The discussion touched on potential future extensions, including support for ZMODP and polynomial rings over other polynomial rings, though these were identified as more complex challenges for future development.

### Grind System Mathematical Operations Issues

The team discussed issues with the Grind system failing to handle certain mathematical operations over rings, particularly when dealing with X to the 4th power. Jay explained that the failure occurred because the system needs to work over fields rather than just general rings, and Matthew suggested rebuilding Grind's infrastructure around algebra types to better handle these cases. The discussion touched on the possibility of using algebra classes to define scalar types and maps between them, though specific implementation details were not fully resolved.

### Proof System Memory Issues Demonstration

Jay demonstrated a proof system he was working on but encountered memory issues when trying to construct and verify complex proofs. He explained that while the system could perform initial rewrites and reach a weak head normal form, it struggled with gluing proofs together completely and handling large expressions. Jay's goal was to create a representation that would make it easier to work with lead terms at the proof level, though he expressed uncertainty about the system's overall utility for his intended purposes.

### Conv Mode Proof Technique Demonstration

Jay demonstrated a proof technique using "conv mode" to manipulate expressions by descending into specific parts of an expression rather than working with the whole expression at once. He showed how this approach could simplify complex expressions, though the process was described as somewhat messy and potentially slow. The discussion revealed that while the technique could rewrite expressions and handle nested rewrites, it didn't yet achieve Jay's goal of making proofs easier to construct, and there were ongoing performance issues with proof construction and checking.

### Lean Kernel Performance Challenges

Jay discussed the performance challenges of using Lean's kernel to prove polynomial simplification theorems, explaining that the current approach creates extremely large expressions that the kernel struggles to process efficiently. He identified that the kernel's unification process, which involves expanding expressions to check for type equality, is particularly slow when handling complex polynomial operations. While Jay noted that the proof construction could potentially be optimized by breaking it into smaller atomic tasks or using call-by-value evaluation, he acknowledged that the current approach of doing full computation at the type level creates unnecessarily large proof objects. The team discussed the possibility of seeing what specific expressions are sent to the kernel for verification, though Matthew indicated this information is typically not stored after type checking is complete.

### Polynomial Proofs and Conference Submission

The team discussed submitting an extended abstract for a conference, with Jay confirming everyone's names were added to the submission. They explored challenges in proving polynomial equality, particularly around polynomial multiplication and expansion in Lean. Jay identified that polynomial multiplication might be a key bottleneck in their current approach. The team agreed to focus on breaking down proofs into smaller steps and potentially using intermediate lemmas to handle larger expressions. They also discussed the need for a medium-sized example to test their approach, rather than relying solely on the current large benchmark. The conversation ended with plans for Anton to make a pull request with a specific example, and the team agreed to continue their discussion in the next meeting, which Michael requested be recorded due to travel constraints.
