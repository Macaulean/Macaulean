## Summary

The team discussed progress on their computer algebra integration project, focusing on both the Lean and Macaulay 2 sides of their implementation. They reviewed AI-generated code attempts and discussed design patterns for abstracting computer algebra tasks, including the need for reusable serialization and connection methods. The group examined existing work on polynomial types and algebra implementations, with Matthew sharing AI-generated specifications for handling polynomial operations and artifact storage. They also discussed the ideal membership problem implementation, deciding to merge the current branch despite some remaining issues, and considered approaches for breaking down complex computations into smaller, formalizable tasks. The conversation touched on potential collaboration with other teams working on similar projects and the possibility of creating tools that could help users formalize proofs from Macaulay 2 computations.

## Topics

### Summer Intern and Code Optimization

Matthew and Damiano discussed an opportunity for a summer intern that Kevin had secured. Matthew then explained his work on abstracting patterns to create reusable components, which he had tested with existing tactics and tests. Jay noted that Claude had created duplicate functions that already existed in Meta, highlighting the models' tendency to be local rather than global optimizers in code. The conversation ended with Matthew preparing to share a design specification in the chat.

### Stored Artifact Feature Discussion

The team discussed a stored artifact feature that Matthew had Cloud and Codex work on, which Jay noted reminded him of a potential enhancement for their extended abstract. Jay proposed the ability to call Macaulay to save computation results and then reference stored files when doing proofs, allowing users to separate computation from proof tasks. The discussion focused on two benefits of this approach: avoiding repeated Macaulay 2 calls for the same problem, and handling large computations that might require distributed processing or take several hours to complete.

### Sage Tactic Implementation Discussion

The team discussed a previous implementation where a tactic interacted with Sage to solve problems, particularly around writing lean code and finding coefficients for linear combinations. Damiano explained that the tactic would ask Sage for coefficients and then replace itself with existing Mathlib tactics, though this approach had limitations as it relied on an online server. Jay noted that serialization slowdowns were primarily on the Macaulay 2 side due to JSON serialization, though Douglas mentioned that performance should improve with the next release featuring a new C library.

### Sage-Lean Certificate Generation Project

Damiano and Anton discussed a project that uses Sage as a backend to provide certificates for formal proofs in Lean, where Sage generates proof terms that Lean's kernel verifies. Jay noted that while their approach can handle reasonable-sized examples, polynomial equality remains a bottleneck for large polynomials in Lean, which Macaulay 2 can easily handle. The discussion focused on comparing their approach to existing methods and identifying performance boundaries.

### Computer Algebra Systems Integration Discussion

The team discussed the implementation of computer algebra systems integration, particularly focusing on Sage's approach and how it might be applied to their needs. They evaluated Sage's implementation, noting that while it handled basic tasks reasonably well, it didn't address their more complex example problems. The group considered creating a specification for ideal membership testing that would focus on polynomials with integer or natural number coefficients as a starting point, with the goal of having performant enough examples to work with before further expanding the functionality. They also discussed potential improvements to their polynomial type implementation and the challenges of working with rational numbers in Lean due to performance considerations in the kernel.

### Tactics Development Progress Update

The team discussed progress on characteristic B and performance development, deciding to merge current work and postpone characteristic P development. Matthew presented test results for Square's tactics, noting its ability to handle decimal and time differences, though Jay warned about potential issues with the Grind proof system. Matthew also mentioned creating a new repository to test tactics with Mathlib's native types, though this project is still in early stages without full Macaulay 2 integration.

### Membership Feature and Component Design

Jay planned to merge the ideal membership feature and work on the extended abstract this week, inviting others to contribute thoughts. Matthew discussed designing a pattern to enable composability and reusability across different components. Anton raised questions about implementing tasks purely in Macaulay 2 and potential integration with Lean, but the discussion ended without a clear conclusion on this topic.

### Macaulay 2 and Lean Integration

The group discussed ways to integrate Macaulay 2 computations with Lean verification. Anton proposed breaking down complex computational tasks into smaller, verifiable components that could be sent to Lean for formal proof. Jay suggested a specific approach for verifying polynomial products by checking terms monomial by monomial. The discussion touched on the challenges of formalizing algorithm verification in Macaulay 2 versus directly implementing and verifying algorithms in Lean. The conversation ended with plans to continue the discussion the following week, though Anton mentioned he would be away camping without internet access for two weeks.
